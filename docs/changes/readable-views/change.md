## Intent
The foundation answers what a view at a scope and a depth shows, but nobody can
look at it yet. After this change an invented reference system as large as a
real one (an online shop of several domains, about thirty services, stores, a
broker, REST and gRPC, external systems, zones and data categories) is drawn
as Mermaid pages and as a LikeC4 workspace: a landscape of about ten boxes, a
view of every domain and of every service with modules, each with its
neighbours outside it, every arrow saying what it carries. The same renderer
serves the server of outcome 4.

Stories:
- As an architect, I open the landscape and see the domains and the external
  systems, with arrows that say what flows between them, not a tangle.
- As an architect, I open a domain and see its services and the other domains
  they talk to, then open a service and see its modules.
- As a model's author, I learn which relations I left without a name before
  anyone reads an unlabelled arrow.
- As a writer of documentation, I paste a Mermaid page that renders on GitHub.

## Out of scope
The server and a view on request (outcome 4); the agent skill (outcome 3);
views per environment or architecture state; zones drawn as boundaries; flows;
a command-line client (the generation script is a development tool, like
`bun run schemas`); output plugins as separate processes; the query engine's
memory (madarch-ti6.2).

## Rationale
The owner asked on 2026-09-25 that no arrow be unlabelled, and that an
unnamed relation be a warning rather than a refusal, so a model written before
names existed still loads. A domain view without the domains it talks to shows
islands, so views take their context as C4 and LikeC4 do.

## Design and decisions
See `design.md` for the mechanism. Decided with the owner on 2026-09-25, in the
preflight:

- Views take their context: neighbours outside the scope, lifted to the
  nearest element beside the scope's line of ancestors.
- Every arrow carries a label; a relation gets an optional `name`; an unnamed
  relation loads with a warning and is labelled by its interface's contract or
  its id.
- Rendering runs in process behind a narrow input (the query engine's answers
  and the compiled model) and is shared by Mermaid, LikeC4 and the future
  server; the glossary gains "renderer", and "output plugin" (a separate
  process) stays for later.
- Views: a landscape, one per element with children; Mermaid pages with
  drill-down links under the diagram (GitHub disables clicks inside diagrams);
  one LikeC4 workspace with the same views.
- The reference system is an invented online shop in
  `examples/reference-system/`, with its rendered views committed beside it
  and regenerated byte-identically by a test.
- No CLI: `bun run views` generates, `bun run views:check` validates (Mermaid's
  parser under happy-dom, `likec4 validate`); both are in `package.json`, and
  CI runs the check. The tools are development dependencies, pinned exactly,
  MIT-licensed, outside the product image.
- Run: subagents of the coordinator's session; Opus 5.5 for every task; review
  by Codex through its CLI.

Checked before writing: `likec4 validate` 1.59.4 runs offline in about a second
and refuses an unresolved reference; `mermaid.parse` 12.0.0 under Bun accepts a
flowchart with frames and labels once a happy-dom global is registered, and
refuses a malformed one.
